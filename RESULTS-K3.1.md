## Verdict
- **K3c (attachment split): killed by one cord.** 63 recto, 69 verso, 1 with unrecorded attachment, against 63 and 70 in Medrano and Urton (2018). Per khipu, UR087, UR088, UR089 and UR091 match their table 1 exactly. UR090 gives 6 recto and 11 verso (published 5 and 12). UR092 gives 9 recto and 1 unrecorded (published 10 and 0). The 133 groups appear, but that count was not blind.
- **K3a (total): killed.** The 133 first cords add up to 749. That is neither 367 (the revisita) nor 467 (the published moiety totals): 382 and 282 away.
- **K3b (moieties): killed.** Verso 458, recto 289, against the published 381 or 382 and 86.

## Claim
From the public OKR v2.1.0 data, with groups reconstructed as six-cord runs, the first-cord values of the Santa khipus do not reproduce the 367-peso total or the published moiety totals. The attachment pattern does reproduce, within one cord.

Credence: moderate. Before calling the published figures wrong, we need to read how Urton (2017, *Inka History in Knots*, pp. 218-235) read these cords.

## Where the gap comes from
- UR090 alone contributes 473. Three of its first cords hold 203, 102 and 105, far above the 0-13 peso range the papers report. Across UR090, most cord values have a hundreds digit and a units digit with an empty tens place (105, 202, 308, 401...). That looks like two fields on one cord, or a recording convention, not a single amount.
- 14 first cords have no knots, 8 of them broken. Missing data lowers the totals by an unknown amount.

## Exploratory, not a claim
Reading only the units digit of every first cord (one reading, chosen after seeing UR090) gives a total of 329, recto 89, verso 238. It does not reproduce 367 or 381/86 either. No other readings were tried.
